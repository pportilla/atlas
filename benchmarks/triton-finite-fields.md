# Finite extension fields on the Radeon 680M

The Atlas CPU, Triton, and Singular 4.4.1 agreed on every coefficient in twelve batches of 256 field elements. On GF(2⁸), Triton multiplication and inversion finished slightly ahead of the Atlas CPU. The CPU completed the other ten Atlas batches faster. Singular recorded the fastest medians in nine cases. Atlas CPU inversion was fastest on GF(5³) and the two large quadratic fields.

|---|---|---:|---:|---:|

| GF(5³) | Add | 1.356 | 1.546 | 0.548 |
| GF(5³) | Subtract | 1.359 | 1.560 | 0.551 |
| GF(5³) | Multiply | 1.408 | 1.572 | 0.607 |
| GF(5³) | Divide | 1.526 | 1.698 | 1.071 |
| GF(5³) | Invert | 0.949 | 1.130 | 1.002 |
| GF(2⁸) | Multiply | 2.866 | 2.823 | 0.647 |
| GF(2⁸) | Invert | 1.925 | 1.856 | 1.238 |
| GF(536870879²) | Multiply | 1.281 | 1.451 | 0.576 |
| GF(536870879²) | Invert | 0.883 | 1.417 | 0.958 |
| GF(2147483647²) | Multiply | 1.319 | 1.488 | 0.814 |
| GF(2147483647²) | Invert | 0.929 | 1.429 | 3.909 |

Each time is the median of seven warmed runs on the Ryzen 7 6800H with integrated Radeon 680M. Atlas timings cover the public dispatcher, coefficient conversion, field validation, arithmetic, and result serialization. The GPU timings include host transfers and require a Triton execution receipt on each run. The driver alternates Atlas CPU and GPU order. Singular times a single-threaded loop over prepared elements in a running process, then checks each result in its own field. For reduction it evaluates unreduced polynomials of degree at most four at the cubic field generator. Process startup, JIT compilation and the checks are outside the timings. [Driver](triton_finite_fields.py) · [Individual trials](triton-finite-fields-680m.json)

On the original GF(2⁸) workloads, switching to a packed binary representation and carryless Triton arithmetic reduced the GPU median for multiplication from 3.241 to 2.823 ms and inversion from 3.458 to 1.856 ms. At this batch size the differences from the optimized Atlas CPU were 0.043 and 0.069 ms respectively.

The field relations are T³+T+1 over GF(5), T⁸+T⁴+T³+T+1 over GF(2), and T²+1 over each large prime field. At characteristic 2³¹−1, Singular's direct algebraic-number inversion reports a characteristic limit of 2²⁹. For the two measurements at that prime the driver uses polynomial representatives, with reduction modulo T²+1 after multiplication. For inversion, Singular lifts 1 to the ideal generated by the input and T²+1 and reduces the input’s Bézout coefficient. [Singular comparison](triton_finite_fields.py)

Run `python benchmarks/triton_finite_fields.py --count 256 --trials 7` with Singular on PATH and a physical Triton device at index zero.
